# Affection and soulmate — brainstorm

Status: mechanics interview resolved through Q71, including the user's final Ring-use preservation override. The user subsequently approved implementation, split feature commits, validation and a PR; see affection-validation.md for actual results. The user accepted the design/display decisions as the phase-3 planning basis and requested `/plan`; see [affection-design.md](./affection-design.md) and [affection-plan.md](./affection-plan.md). This remains the product-decision/interview record, not implementation authorization. Both reward tables are settled initial-release values. The user has selected a compact next-reward meter for the initial display and excluded a paged reward path; detailed styling can follow later. No affection implementation, tests, migrations, deployment, or commits are authorized by these interview answers.

## Purpose

Give players meaningful progression with favorite characters after acquisition. Affection alone does not create unowned characters or resolve ownership saturation.

## Settled decisions

- Affection belongs to a player–character pair within one server. Ownership loss preserves it; a new owner uses their own progress. Reacquisition restores access to the former owner's saved progress.
- A player can select one currently owned character as their soulmate. Every successful selection starts a 12-hour voluntary switching cooldown, including initial selection and replacement after ownership loss. Losing ownership permits selecting a replacement immediately regardless of an existing cooldown; the replacement starts a new 12-hour cooldown.
- Selection confirmation rechecks ownership, cooldown permission, and character availability. A character busy in an escape, trade/gift offer, or item challenge cannot be newly selected until that interaction ends. A stale or canceled confirmation changes nothing.
- Rolling an already owned character grants the roller a large direct affection award for that character, without requiring reclaim or starting an escape attempt. This applies to owned characters other than the soulmate too.
- Soulbound characters are eligible for their owner's rolls, which award affection without starting an escape attempt. They remain excluded from other players' ordinary rolls.
- Own-character appearances do not trigger Cupid's Misfire, including owner-eligible soulbound appearances. They grant normal affection and leave Misfire armed for a later roll of someone else's character. This explicitly supersedes the item's previous "even your own" behavior.
- Successfully reclaiming a character on another player's roll grants the owner a medium direct award for that character.
- A qualifying related-character roll grants a small indirect award to the soulmate without requiring marriage or reclaim. Franchise matching and genre matching are accepted; genre awards are a smaller fallback when there is no franchise match.
- One shared genre across either character's associated anime qualifies. Use the distinct genres from all associated anime; multiple shared genres still grant only one genre award. Never infer missing genre metadata.
- Missing-genre fairness exception (Q47 accepted): if the selected soulmate has no genre metadata, each successfully shown character on the owner's rolls grants a fallback one affection when no higher award applies. This supersedes skipping all fallback affection for a soulmate with missing genres; it does not assign invented genres. A genre-covered soulmate does not receive this exception merely because the rolled character has no genre metadata. Direct and franchise awards remain eligible.
- Only the soulmate owner's rolls grant indirect affection. Other players' franchise or genre matches do not.
- Golden Whistle's automatic successful reclaim on another player's roll earns the normal medium direct award.
- Successful reclaims of item-funded Siren's Call escapes also earn the normal 20 affection, including Golden Whistle reclaims. Siren activation grants no indirect affection to its user and has no ordinary roll cost for Windfall to preserve.
- Accepted base awards are 100 for rolling one's own character, 20 for a successful reclaim on someone else's roll, and 1 for a genre match or the accepted no-genre-soulmate fallback. Q59 confirms the new franchise scoring preserves higher awards for smaller franchises: 1–5 published characters awards 12, 6–10 awards 10, and 11+ awards 5. These replace the previous 4/3/2 scores. The lowest-award-among-shared-franchises rule remains settled.
- For any one roll and player–character pair, use the highest qualifying award category rather than adding awards. First resolve the franchise category by taking the lowest size-based award among shared franchises; compare that single result with other qualifying categories. The lowest-franchise rule does not reduce direct own-character or reclaim awards. Multiple shared genres do not multiply a genre award.
- Appearance-award eligibility snapshots the selected soulmate, own-character ownership, matching data, and Unbroken Bond chance at character selection. Reclaim awards snapshot the owner's active perks when reclaim succeeds. Switching during a pending roll cannot redirect appearance affection, and a perk unlocked by an award cannot boost that same award.
- Appearance affection is earned only after the character is successfully shown. A hidden mystery introduction alone earns nothing; its character reveal qualifies even after a loss or when the prize is unclaimable. Failed delivery earns nothing unless a retry successfully shows the character. Canceled appearances earn nothing. Successful retries award at most once.
- Q67 is accepted: honor a saved appearance award for the original player–character pair even if ownership or soulmate selection changes before successful presentation, unless the appearance itself is canceled. Do not redirect or recalculate it; saved progress can increase without restoring ownership-dependent passives.
- Item-boosted rolls qualify normally. Franchise-size adjustments must be bounded, including when Franchise Passport concentrates the roll pool.
- Reward-use cooldowns and earned reward limits belong to the player. Switching or reacquiring a soulmate does not refresh them.
- Switching recalculates benefits immediately while preserving spent allowances and existing cooldowns; it never grants a fresh allowance.
- Normal character affection rewards include one-time item payouts, stacking bonuses to hourly roll capacity, and stacking reductions to the wait for another normal claim without changing the server's reset schedule. Across the player's currently owned harem, these passive bonuses cap at +5 rolls per hourly allowance and a one-hour reduction in the wait after the last normal claim; an earlier scheduled claim reset still wins. Permanent targeting bonuses are excluded from the initial release. The normal payout track is recorded below; fixed Wish Upon A Star at 1,800 is replaced by a random Legendary. The user requires a normal milestone every 100 affection from 200 onward and approved the later Epic/Epic/Legendary pattern from 1,000 through the 2,100 cap. A soulmate receives the same normal rewards and additionally provides player-wide soulmate checkpoint perks.
- A sufficiently developed character contributes +1 hourly roll capacity and six minutes of normal claim-wait reduction at separate normal milestones, with the roll-capacity contribution unlocked before the claim-wait reduction. Five qualifying owned characters reach the +5 roll-capacity ceiling; ten reach the one-hour claim-wait ceiling. The accepted normal thresholds are 200 for the roll-capacity contribution and 700 for claim-wait reduction.
- Add normal affection roll capacity after Adrenaline Rush's multiplier: with base seven, 2× Rush, and +1 affection capacity, the allowance is 15, not 16. Preserve spent usage through capacity changes.
- Normal affection claim reduction and Engagement Ring do not add together. Use the earliest claim availability from the scheduled reset, affection reduction, or Ring. Engagement Ring normally spends a prepared early-claim use only when it actually enables a successful marriage that would otherwise still be unavailable; Claim Conservation can preserve that use under the explicit exception below.
- Q69 final user decision: when Engagement Ring enables an otherwise-unavailable successful marriage and Claim Conservation triggers, preserve both the normal claim and the Ring's enabling prepared early-claim use with the same conservation check. This supersedes the recommendation to spend the Ring counter on a conserved marriage. It does not refund the inventory charge previously paid to activate the Ring, add another Ring use, extend expiry, or create a second conservation check. The Ring spends no prepared use if affection reduction or the scheduled reset already permits the marriage.
- Unlocked soulmate checkpoints are cumulative: the active soulmate supplies all checkpoint perks their saved affection has unlocked.
- Soulmate checkpoint perks are exclusive effects absent from normal character milestones. They may be percentage-based passives whose chance increases at later checkpoints; they need not introduce new triggered gameplay systems.
- Normal character passives require current ownership. They stop on ownership loss and resume on reacquisition. One-time milestone payouts remain earned and cannot be earned again by reacquiring the character.
- Attack negation is exclusive to the active soulmate and protects the player's whole harem. The earlier decision assigning negation to normal characters is superseded.
- Stacking passives have visible effect-specific ceilings. Every character shares the same normal milestone track and soulmate checkpoint track; perks are not assigned randomly per character.
- Only the currently selected, owned soulmate provides soulmate checkpoint perks. Switching soulmates or losing ownership removes the old soulmate's checkpoint perks; the newly selected soulmate provides whichever checkpoint perks their saved affection has unlocked.
- Ownership loss clears the soulmate selection. Reacquiring a former soulmate preserves their affection but never automatically reselects them or replaces the current soulmate. Selecting them again follows the normal selection rules.
- Items already awarded and temporary rewards already activated remain earned after switching soulmates or ownership loss, until their normal consumption or expiry. Only ongoing character-dependent passives stop; switching grants no fresh allowance or cooldown reset.
- Competitive abilities should remain moderate rather than game-changing. The earlier exclusive active-ability loadout and prohibition on stacking are superseded by the selected automatic perk roster, percentages, and visible caps below.
- Earlier reward candidates included extra rolls, improved targeting, claim flexibility, item access, and attack negation. The selected normal track and seven-perk soulmate roster below supersede this candidate list; permanent targeting is not selected for the initial normal track.
- Negation covers eligible single-target theft and forced-divorce attacks. It excludes ordinary escape marriages, mass attacks, and soulbind removal.
- Negating Heart for a Heart stops the whole action: both characters remain owned and no sacrifice occurs. The attack item charge is normally spent, subject to Item Conservation.
- Hostage Note receives one Negation check when its validated challenge opens, before Shield or Crown. Success prevents the entire challenge, including exchange/divorce potential, and normally spends the Note. Opening is this attack's resolution boundary: if unblocked, later challenge settlement never checks Negation again, even if the defender changes soulmates.
- Siren's Call activation is explicitly eligible for Negation before Crown. Success normally spends the Siren charge but creates no escape. If unblocked, the escape proceeds normally with no new Negation check at publication or a later marriage. This does not broaden protection to ordinary escape marriages.
- Negating another player's Cupid's Misfire exhausts the armed Misfire effect, prevents its divorce, and retains ownership. The rolled character then follows ordinary escape behavior; Negation does not suppress natural escape attempts. Own-character appearances still skip Misfire and retain it as previously accepted.
- After validating an eligible attack, check negation before existing defensive items. Successful negation preserves Mirror Shield and Counterfeit Crown; the attack item is normally consumed. Otherwise existing defenses apply.
- Determine the active soulmate's protection at attack resolution and resolve each attack only once; retries cannot reroll negation.
- All seven soulmate perks initially unlock at an 8% chance. Windfall's 8% start supersedes its earlier accepted 5% start. Each perk has two later chance upgrades: Windfall 8% → 10% → 15%; Negation 8% → 12% → 16%; Item Conservation, Fortune's Favor, Echo, Unbroken Bond, and Claim Conservation each 8% → 16% → 20%. The user's 20% ceiling supersedes the proposed 24% ceiling for those five perks. These are the accepted initial release tracks, with cumulative checkpoint thresholds in the accepted table below.
- Higher checkpoint percentages for the same effect replace the earlier percentage rather than adding to it. Distinct checkpoint effects remain cumulative.
- Different perks use independent checks and may trigger on the same event. Favor can upgrade an item and Echo can duplicate that upgraded result; neither changes the other's chance. Bonus copies never start another perk chain.
- Item conservation preserves only the charge the use would spend. It does not duplicate the item, refresh expiry, or restore older charges.
- Conservation applies to automatic and defensive inventory-charge expenditures, including Golden Whistle, Mirror Shield, and Counterfeit Crown's activation charge. Each charge expenditure receives one check; retries do not repeat it.
- Conservation preserves only the source item's inventory use charge, not prepaid buff counters or other costs. Crown receives its check when activated, not again when redirecting. Passport receives a check on activation, not on each targeting use. Crafting ingredients, sacrificed characters, whole-entry discards/replacements, and expiry are never preserved. Claim Conservation separately handles the claim resources it covers.
- Conservation eligibility uses the active soulmate and perk chance at valid item-effect commitment, immediately before its consequences, not when a menu opens. An action that sacrifices its own funding soulmate may still conserve its source item charge, never the sacrifice. Delayed delivery/recovery reuses the saved result; provisional-debit finalization must preserve the existing refund/expiry rules without granting a second check.
- Conservation also applies to an attack item consumed by a blocked attack. Preserving the charge does not change the failed attack outcome.
- The selected soulmate checkpoint roster is Negation, Item Conservation, Fortune's Favor, Echo, Unbroken Bond, Claim Conservation, and Windfall. Other proposed candidates are not selected.
- All unlocked checkpoint perks apply automatically while the soulmate is selected and owned. There is no individual perk equipment selection.
- Fortune's Favor can upgrade an earned random item by one rarity tier; Echo can grant an additional copy of an eligible item reward; Unbroken Bond can double an affection award once; Claim Conservation can preserve the claim resource normally spent by a successful marriage; Windfall can make a qualifying roll cost zero. The accepted chances, sources, ordering, and boundaries are specified in the decisions and tables below.
- Fortune's Favor upgrades at most to Legendary. Legendary and ??? results remain unchanged. An upgrade selects uniformly from reward-eligible item kinds in the next rarity tier rather than using item-specific counterparts. If that tier has no eligible items, retain the original item.
- Fortune's Favor resolves before Echo, which duplicates the final item. Bonus copies never trigger another upgrade or duplication.
- Existing vault gambling and Recast decisions finish before Fortune's Favor and Echo: existing vault decisions, then Favor, then Echo, then delivery. A lost gamble leaves no reward to upgrade or duplicate.
- Echo copies are delivered independently. With one free inventory slot, acquire one copy and retain the other as pending overflow. Each pending copy can be replaced, accepted later, or declined without removing an acquired copy. Each copy's normal lifetime starts when it is acquired.
- Fortune's Favor and Echo initially apply to vault and Consolation Chit random item rewards. Crafting, gifts, trades, and all affection milestone payouts, whether fixed or random, are excluded.
- Favor/Echo eligibility and percentages are fixed when the player selects their vault reward, or when a Chit triggers. They resolve after existing reward decisions. Later switching, ownership loss, progression, or delayed delivery does not change that reward's saved perk eligibility or chances, and retries cannot reroll them.
- Claim Conservation preserves whichever resource the successful marriage would spend: the normal claim, an extra claim token, or a temporary free claim. Preserving the normal claim leaves its cooldown unchanged. If Engagement Ring is needed to enable that normal claim, the same successful check also preserves the Ring's prepared early-claim use as an explicit exception for Claim Conservation, not Item Conservation. Each marriage receives at most one conservation check.
- Unbroken Bond doubles a qualifying affection award once, including franchise matches, reclaims, and own-character appearances. The active soulmate supplies the perk, which can also boost awards to other owned characters. It never triggers itself.
- Windfall makes the current qualifying roll cost zero: no roll is spent. It does not grant a bonus roll or start an automatic roll. Every otherwise qualifying separately requested roll receives an independent Windfall check, including consecutive rolls after a free roll.
- Windfall requires at least one available roll before a roll can be requested. It can preserve an available roll, but cannot be attempted with zero remaining rolls.
- Windfall is the first soulmate-exclusive perk to unlock at 100 affection, starting at an 8% chance with no additional daily use limit. The user's revised 100 threshold supersedes both the earlier accepted 150 and proposed 175. One qualifying own-character appearance can therefore unlock it from zero; the newly unlocked perk affects only later events. Later checkpoints increase it to 10% and 15%. Unbroken Bond is not the first perk.
- Accepted soulmate checkpoint interleaving (all cumulative thresholds are accepted in the table below): Windfall 8%, Item Conservation 8%, Unbroken Bond 8%, Windfall 10%, Claim Conservation 8%, Item Conservation 16%, Fortune's Favor 8%, Unbroken Bond 16%, Negation 8%, Claim Conservation 16%, Echo 8%, Fortune's Favor 16%, Negation 12%, Echo 16%, Windfall 15%, Item Conservation 20%, Unbroken Bond 20%, Claim Conservation 20%, Fortune's Favor 20%, Negation 16%, Echo 20%. This preserves the accepted new-perk order while interleaving upgrades.
- Earlier pacing targets were 4–6 weeks for all seven base perks and 10–12 weeks for every upgrade. The user subsequently requested lower affection requirements throughout progression, not just the first checkpoint, based on observed difficulty rolling same-franchise characters. Faster progression now takes precedence; do not preserve those older week targets by keeping expensive later thresholds. The user approved the reduced schedule below: all seven base perks at 900 and all upgrades at 2,100.
- Affection is capped at the final available reward's threshold. An award that would exceed it is clamped and excess is discarded, not banked. If the track expands later, progress can resume from the old cap without previously discarded excess.
- An affection award that crosses multiple milestones grants every newly crossed reward once. Apply the award in full up to the final threshold, without requiring separate rolls for each unlock. Perks unlocked by that award apply only to later events.
- Every player–character pair starts at zero affection when the feature launches, including existing ownership. Do not backfill marriage age, historical rolls, or reclaims. Existing owners may select a soulmate immediately.
- Q71 is accepted: do not reconstruct missing selection-time affection/perk eligibility for pre-feature pending rolls or rewards from current state. An event without the required saved eligibility receives no new processing that depends on that missing snapshot. A genuinely post-launch independent event, such as a successful reclaim, uses its own accepted commitment boundary normally.
- Each separate qualifying roll awards full affection even if the same character appears repeatedly; there is no additional daily affection cap or repeat-character penalty. Targeting items may legitimately accelerate progress. Reprocessing the same source event never awards extra affection.
- Windfall applies to every valid player-requested roll that normally spends a roll, including character, mystery, vault, prank, and escape outcomes. Rejected requests spend nothing and receive no Windfall check.
- Windfall's cost decision is final when the valid roll request is accepted, even if Discord delivery later fails. A triggered Windfall remains free; otherwise existing delivery-failure cost rules remain unchanged. Retries neither spend another roll nor reroll Windfall.

## Code facts affecting open decisions

- Franchise matching can reuse canonical franchise membership; a character can belong to multiple franchises.
- Genre data belongs to anime. Characters can have several anime associations or no genre metadata.
- Existing code excludes soulbound characters from ordinary rolls. The accepted owner-only eligibility change above requires implementation.
- Golden Whistle automatically performs a successful reclaim, qualifying for affection under the accepted rule above.
- Existing roll selection has no persistent recent-character cooldown. Small available pools and targeting items permit repeated matches.
- Current vault reward flow is optional Fortune's Lens, choice/reveal, optional Double or Nothing, optional Fortune's Recast, then inventory delivery. An initially revealed item can still change or be lost before delivery. Chit rewards bypass these vault modifiers. See lib/vaultSessions.js, lib/vaultFortune.js, lib/vaultRewards.js, and lib/mysteryConsolation.js.
- Existing vault/Chit delivery handles one item, not a bundle. Full inventory preserves the reward as pending overflow; replacement, later acceptance, and decline are supported. Overflow survives restart without rerolling and has no inventory expiry deadline. Accepted Echo/milestone behavior reuses that interaction per earned copy, extending provenance and delivery accounting as needed, not introducing another reward dialog.
- Existing getRewardItemsInTier resolves the guild's reward-eligible definitions for one rarity, and existing item selection is uniform within a selected tier. Direct reward creation currently validates Chit-specific provenance, so milestone reuse requires extending source handling rather than treating the existing helper as already source-generic. These are local source facts from lib/itemRoster.js and lib/vaultRewards.js, not implemented affection changes.
- Each acquired copy occupies its own inventory slot. Its lifetime starts at inventory insertion using its acquired rarity tier and the then-current expiry configuration, not when the reward is revealed. Spending an item's final charge can free a slot; conserving that charge may keep the slot occupied. See lib/itemStorage.js and lib/itemExpiry.js.
- Built-in roll defaults are seven rolls per player per guild in fixed hourly buckets, shared across gender pools and channels. Unused capacity does not accumulate. Daily resets current-bucket usage rather than banking extra rolls. Prank and vault replacements also cost a roll but produce no character appearance. These are source defaults, not verified live-server settings. See lib/gameConfigSchema.js and lib/characterRolls.js.
- The normal claim uses fixed three-hour buckets, not a rolling three-hour cooldown after each marriage. Successful marriages spend resources in order: Double Date free claim, Crown token, Piñata token, then normal claim. Reclaims spend none. Engagement Ring can enable an earlier claim without changing the bucket schedule. New claim passives need explicit composition rules rather than changing that schedule.
- Adrenaline Rush doubles roll capacity while preserving spent usage; refills reset usage without banking rolls. Any normal affection capacity bonus needs explicit ordering with Rush. Configuration resolves built-in defaults, then global overrides, then guild overrides; active item buffs can retain snapshotted rules.
- Source inspection cannot establish actual player activity or typical affection earned per day. The accepted balancing baseline is approximately 57 rolls per active day. These three size bands supersede both earlier five-band proposals. The revised 12/10/5 awards replace the previous 4/3/2 awards without increasing progression thresholds. Shares describe franchise groups, not unique characters or roll probabilities.

Accepted counting policy: retain all canonical franchise memberships; do not force a primary franchise or split a character's weight between franchises. Count each published character once within each franchise they belong to. Use whole published franchise counts, not the currently available, gender-filtered, or item-concentrated roll pool. For a roll, intersect the soulmate's and rolled character's franchise memberships; only shared franchises qualify. Evaluate each shared franchise's size-based award and grant the lowest single award without adding them. This supersedes the proposed highest-shared-franchise rule: sharing a twelve-point and a five-point franchise grants five franchise affection, not twelve or seventeen. A franchise belonging only to one side cannot affect the award; do not add the sizes of shared franchises or use a union of all memberships as the sizing denominator. After resolving the franchise category, the existing highest-qualifying-category rule still applies, so an own-character direct award remains 100 rather than being reduced to the franchise amount. Q25/Q25a size bands and overlap handling are settled.

This bounded compensation does not equalize progress: large franchises can still yield more affection over time through more frequent matches. A one-character franchise has no different related character to roll; rolling the owned character itself uses the higher direct award. The first checkpoint at 100 and the full reduced schedule are accepted. Any refreshed pacing estimates must include genre/direct-match behavior and the revised franchise scores rather than relying on franchise counts alone.
